Statistical Evaluation

Mean Absolute Error (MAE) & Model Formulation

"Mean Absolute Error measures the average absolute difference between actual and predicted water consumption. Lower MAE generally indicates better predictions."

Rigorous Train-Test Validation

To ensure true predictive validity and avoid fabricated metrics, the system performs historical back-testing:

  • The dataset is split into a training segment (earliest months) and a test segment (last 6 historical months).
  • The model fits its trend and seasonality strictly on the training partition.
  • Forecasts are generated for the test months and compared against known actual records using:
    MAE = (1 / n) × Σ |Actual(i) - Predicted(i)|
  • Current Test Set MAE: 1.67 Megaliters (MAPE: 0.28%).

College Project Documentation

Urban Water Consumption Analysis and Forecasting System

Academic project documentation detailing system architecture, mathematical methodology, technology stack, and municipal applications.

System Objective

Rapid urbanization and climate variability place unprecedented strain on municipal water utilities. Traditional water governance often relies on static historical averages, leading to reservoir shortages during unexpected heat waves or excessive pumping costs during low-demand periods.

The primary objective of the Urban Water Consumption Analysis and Forecasting System is to build a robust, scalable, and interpretable analytical system that:

  • Ingests real-world monthly municipal water consumption time-series data.
  • Separates underlying long-term structural demographic growth from cyclical annual seasonal patterns.
  • Produces an accurate 6-month prospective demand outlook to guide water authority procurement and reservoir management.
  • Validates model reliability using real historical test sets with Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE).
  • Provides an intuitive, responsive dashboard interface accessible to municipal planners and non-technical stakeholders.

Mathematical & Algorithmic Methodology

The system utilizes Classical Time-Series Additive Decomposition comprising three sequential stages:

1

Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) Trend Analysis

Calculates the linear baseline trajectory across time indices x = [0, 1, ..., N-1]. Slope β₁ = Σ(x - x̄)(y - ȳ) / Σ(x - x̄)² and Intercept β₀ = ȳ - β₁x̄ establish whether the city exhibits an expanding or declining consumption trend.

2

Monthly Seasonal Residual Decomposition

For each observation, the linear trend is subtracted to compute monthly residuals: Residual(t) = Actual(t) - Trend(t). Residuals are grouped by calendar month (1 to 12), averaged, and normalized around zero to yield pure seasonal coefficients.

3

Out-of-Sample Forecasting & Back-Testing

Future projections are synthesized: Forecast(t) = β₀ + β₁·(N + offset) + Seasonal_Effect(Month). Validation splits the data into training (historical up to N-6) and testing (final 6 months) to calculate real MAE.

Real-World Municipal Applications

Reservoir Storage Planning

Enables water boards to anticipate seasonal drawdowns and regulate inter-basin water transfers before reservoir depletion occurs.

Pumping Energy Optimization

Utilities can schedule energy-intensive water pumping during off-peak electricity tariff hours based on accurate daily/monthly demand projections.

Drought & Scarcity Preparedness

Provides early warning indicators when observed consumption significantly exceeds predicted baseline thresholds.

Infrastructure Budgeting

Provides municipal city councils with mathematically sound 5-to-10 year expansion justifications for water treatment plant expansions.
